Inside the reservoir survey

By Mina Okafor and Jules Martin

Samples were collected from twelve shoreline sites during the dry season. The team used the same sealed containers at every location.

Each site was visited at the same hour on three consecutive days. Researchers measured water temperature before opening a container and recorded wind, cloud cover, and recent rainfall. These observations helped the laboratory distinguish a local reading from a change that affected the whole reservoir.

Independent technicians repeated a quarter of the measurements and found no material variance.

The largest difference appeared near the western boat launch after a busy holiday weekend. A follow-up visit two days later found that the value had returned to its normal range. The report therefore lists the result as a temporary event instead of a persistent shoreline trend.

Survey leaders will repeat the route during spring runoff. That round will use the same site identifiers and handling procedure, which lets reviewers compare seasons without adjusting for a new sampling design.
